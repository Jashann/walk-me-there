The biggest temple I ever saw was in Nara, on our trip to Japan. I don't remember its name, it was a big old one on the edge of the park.
You come in on a straight path of big flat stones, with white raked gravel on both sides, and two tall stone lanterns, one on each side of the path.
Then the path goes over a pond full of lotus, pink flowers and big round leaves, on a red wooden bridge that is arched very high in the middle, so you climb up and then down.
The railings of the bridge were bright red with little gold caps on top of every post.
Straight ahead after the bridge is the main hall, huge, maybe thirty metres wide, two floors, red pillars and dark wooden lattice doors, and white walls up high.
It sits up on a stone platform with steps, and the roof is enormous, dark grey tiles, curving up at the corners, with two gold ornaments on the ends of the ridge and little bronze bells hanging from the corners.
Under the roof there are all these layers of wooden brackets, red and dark, really intricate.
On the left of the main hall is a pagoda, five floors, each with its own roof, and a gold spire on the top with rings.
On the right is a smaller hall with the same red pillars and a heavy tiled roof.
There was a cherry tree in blossom by the main hall and a twisted old pine on the other side, and behind everything the hills were covered in trees.
